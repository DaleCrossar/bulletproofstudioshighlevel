# Forever Strong Fitness: reel learnings

Running notes on reels we've studied and what we take from them for the Airdrie reels. Newest first. For Dale and David.

## 16 November 2026: PrimeFit, "After the age of 30" (bone density)

Link: https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dfx05mmxSNk/

Account: @getprimefitapp (a training app). Posted 15 November 2026. 95 seconds. 553 likes and 11 comments one day in. Views and follower count weren't available, so we can't say whether this is an outlier for them. Treat it as a style reference, not proof that the format wins.

It covers the same trial as our Reel 06 (LIFTMOR), which makes it a direct comparison.

### What it is

A fully animated explainer. No face, no gym footage. One voice runs from start to finish over white line drawings on black, with a single blue accent colour.

### How it's built

| Time | Beat | What is said | What is on screen |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0 to 4s | Claim | "After the age of 30, resistance training is one of your best defences against bone decline." | A line-drawn figure at a dumbbell rack. An age counter runs up to 30. |
| 4 to 12s | Open loop | "Here's what most people don't realise." Bones start losing density around 30, long before symptoms. | The top of the thigh bone in a circle, filling in then thinning as the age counter runs. |
| 12 to 17s | Risk number | 1 in 2 women and up to 1 in 4 men over 50 will break a bone. | Figures standing on a map. One in two turns blue. |
| 18 to 29s | How it works | Bone is living tissue. Some cells remove it, others build it. With age, removal wins. | A close-up of the bone surface with cells digging and filling. Age counter 20, then 60. |
| 29 to 45s | What isn't enough | Walking, yoga and Pilates are good for you, but on their own may not load bone enough. | The same figure walking, doing yoga, on a reformer. A strain gauge dial barely moves. |
| 45 to 52s | Why lifting differs | A heavy load pushes through bone, cells sense it and signal building. | The figure pressing a barbell. The dial swings. Cells light up. |

### What makes the style work

1. **The picture shows the mechanism, not just the number.** We see bone thin out, cells dig and refill, a dial move under load. By the time the trial result arrives, the viewer already understands why it would happen.
2. **One figure, one world, no cuts.** The same line-drawn body walks, lifts, balances and ages. Scenes morph into each other, so nothing feels like a slide change. Scene detection found no hard cuts in 95 seconds.
3. **Two colours.** White lines on black, blue only for the thing being talked about (the bone, the muscle working, the key number). The eye always knows where to look.
4. **Circle call-outs.** While the body does the exercise, a circle beside it shows the bone or spine responding. Action and effect are in the same frame.
5. **A running age counter.** "AGE 30", "AGE 60", "AGE 80" in small type. Time passing is shown, never explained.
6. **Captions are small and steady.** Top left, one to four words at a time, the key word in blue. They never cover the drawing. A thin progress bar sits above them.
7. **It's kind about walking and yoga.** "Offer important health benefits, but on their own..." It doesn't tell viewers what they already do is useless.
8. **One line to remember before the ask.** "Your bones don't just need movement, they need resistance."
9. **The post caption does real work.** Keyword ask first, then the whole argument in numbered points, then named sources, then a specific share line: "Send this to someone over 30 who only walks for exercise."

### What we take for Forever Strong

1. **Add a "how it works" beat to the Evidence reels.** Our order is hook, lock-in, study, rehook, meaning, ask. Put two or three lines of mechanism between the lock-in and the study. For Reel 06, in our voice: "Bone is living tissue. Your body is always taking old bone away and building new bone. As we get older, the taking away starts to win."
2. **Make the GRAPHIC shots show the body, not only the figure on screen.** Where a graphic currently reads "+2.9%", show a spine section getting denser with the number beside it. Where it reads "−0.95kg", show the leg muscle shrinking.
3. **Use one drawn figure across all the Evidence graphics.** Same style, same accent colour, so reels 02 to 06 look like a series.
4. **Use the age counter for the physical pension.** A figure ageing from 50 to 75 while still getting off the floor is the pension idea in one shot. It suits reels 05 and 09.
5. **Shrink the captions over graphics.** One to four words, one position, key word in our accent colour. Keep the big headline text for the FACE shots.
6. **Stay kind about what people already do.** Use the "good for you, but on its own" form whenever we compare lifting with walking, classes or swimming.
7. **Put the sources and a numbered summary in the post caption.** We already name the study out loud. The caption should carry journal, year and the three or four key points for people who read instead of watch.
8. **Make the share line as specific as theirs.** "Send this to someone who only walks for exercise" beats a general "send this to a friend".

### What we don't copy

1. **No face.** They sell an app. We sell a room in Airdrie and the two people who run it. Dale or David stays on camera for the hook, the turn and the ask. One fully animated reel is worth a test, but it isn't the default.
2. **Rounded numbers and an unnamed study.** They say "an Australian trial", "about 3%" and "about 1%". We say LIFTMOR, 2018, +2.9% and −1.2%, and that every session was coached. Our rule holds.
3. **US figures.** "1 in 2 women and up to 1 in 4 men" is an American statistic. If we want a risk number, find the UK one from the Royal Osteoporosis Society and check it before it goes in a script.
4. **"After 30".** Their audience is everyone over 30. Ours is over 50.
5. **The length.** 95 seconds holds because the picture never stops moving. Our talking head reels should stay shorter unless the graphics carry as much.
